# Clinical model linkage to Budget Impact Analysis

Version 0.10 links the platform's existing clinical models to Budget Impact Analysis (BIA) without turning the BIA into a duplicate clinical model.

## Purpose

The clinical model and BIA answer different parts of the decision problem:

- the clinical model estimates what happens to a treated patient over time and the condition-related costs generated by those outcomes;
- the BIA estimates the financial consequences for a defined budget holder as population size and treatment utilisation change over annual budget periods.

The linkage therefore transfers a **per-patient annual condition-related cost trajectory** from a clinical model into the payer-population calculation.

Supported clinical sources are:

- Decision Tree Modeller;
- Cohort Markov Modeller;
- Advanced Markov / semi-Markov dynamics.

## Explicit cost selection and double-counting control

The modeller must explicitly choose which clinical-model cost-reward parameters are imported into BIA. The software does not automatically import every cost parameter.

For state-transition models, unselected clinical cost rewards are set to zero for the linkage projection. For decision trees, only selected timed cost rewards contribute to the projected annual cost profile.

In v0.10, imported clinical costs **replace** the BIA `disease_management` / condition-related-care category. The following BIA categories remain direct payer inputs unless a future linkage mode is deliberately added:

- acquisition / technology;
- administration / procedure;
- monitoring / follow-up;
- adverse-event management;
- other budgeted costs or credits.

This replacement rule is deliberate: adding the clinical disease-management trajectory on top of the BIA disease-management input would double count the same cost category.

## Population basis: annual treatment starts

A multi-year clinical trajectory creates an important stock-versus-flow distinction.

The annual eligible population in a conventional BIA can be interpreted as a cross-sectional stock. A linked clinical trajectory, however, needs to know when each patient cohort starts treatment so Year-1, Year-2 and later costs can be assigned to the correct budget year.

Version 0.10 therefore requires the modeller to confirm that the annual BIA population values represent **new treatment starts / newly initiating patients** for the linked analysis.

Each annual cohort:

1. is split between interventions using that year's current or future treatment mix;
2. is mapped to a clinical-model strategy;
3. retains that strategy for its projected clinical trajectory;
4. contributes first-year clinical costs in its initiation year;
5. contributes later-year clinical costs in subsequent budget periods.

The cohorts are stacked across budget years. The software does not silently treat a prevalence stock as a fresh incident cohort every year.

A future extension may support prevalent-stock models by explicitly describing time-since-treatment / state distributions for the prevalent population. That is not inferred in v0.10.

## State-transition models

For Cohort Markov and Advanced Markov models:

- the existing transition structure, mortality logic, state-time/model-time behaviour and reward accrual are retained;
- the model is rerun with **zero cost discounting**, because BIA reports annual cash-flow consequences rather than discounted present values in the current reference profiles;
- unselected clinical cost rewards are set to zero;
- selected cost rewards are projected as per-patient cycle costs;
- cycle costs are aggregated into annual cost trajectories.

For annual aggregation, the clinical cycle length must be no longer than one year and must divide one year exactly (for example 12, 6, 3 or 1 month cycles). The software blocks unsupported cycle lengths rather than assigning partial cycles to years heuristically.

The clinical model horizon must be long enough to cover the BIA trajectory horizon. The software does not silently assume zero clinical cost after a shorter clinical-model horizon.

## Decision-tree models

Decision-tree cost rewards already carry absolute timing from model start. The linkage therefore uses branch probabilities to calculate expected path-weighted costs and places selected timed costs into annual budget periods.

The annual timing convention is explicit:

- `t = 0` through `t = 1` year is Budget Year 1;
- times after year 1 through year 2 are Budget Year 2;
- and so on.

Decision-tree rewards beyond the selected BIA horizon are not included in the BIA horizon.

## Currency and perspective

Selected clinical cost parameters must use the same currency as the BIA. A mismatch blocks the linked run rather than relabelling values.

The clinical projection also applies the active clinical model's included cost-bearer perspective. Users remain responsible for ensuring that the clinical cost perspective is appropriate for the BIA budget holder.

## Strategy mapping

Every BIA intervention / option must map explicitly to one clinical-model strategy. The mapping is displayed in the linkage output.

Multiple BIA options may map to the same clinical strategy when clinically appropriate, but any cost differences that are not represented by the shared clinical trajectory must remain explicit in the direct BIA cost inputs.

## Outputs

The linked workspace reports:

- annual direct current-scenario cost;
- annual linked clinical current-scenario cost;
- total current-scenario cost;
- annual direct future-scenario cost;
- annual linked clinical future-scenario cost;
- total future-scenario cost;
- annual net budget impact;
- cumulative budget impact;
- PMPM where covered lives are supplied;
- per-patient annual clinical cost trajectories;
- detailed stacked initiation cohorts.

The output also records the selected clinical cost parameters and the BIA-to-clinical strategy mapping for transparency.

## Current boundary

Version 0.10 links **financial clinical consequences** from existing models. It does not yet import natural resource units such as infusion-chair hours, bed-days, clinician time, diagnostic tests or device quantities. That is the next planned development layer: capacity and resource planning.

Richer BIA scenario and uncertainty management is intentionally scheduled after the capacity/resource layer so uncertainty can cover both financial and physical resource constraints coherently.
