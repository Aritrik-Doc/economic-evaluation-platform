# Population, uptake and clinical resource linkage — v0.14

## Purpose

Version 0.14 separates three concepts that were previously coupled through the Budget Impact Analysis workflow:

1. **Population and uptake** — how many people are relevant to the policy decision and how they are allocated across current and future options;
2. **Clinical resource consequences** — natural-unit resource use generated by the clinical pathway for one patient/cohort;
3. **Capacity** — the physical service capacity available to meet that demand.

Budget Impact Analysis remains a financial affordability model. Resource & Capacity Planning no longer requires a BIA to have been completed.

## Shared Population & Uptake layer

The `Population & Uptake` workspace defines:

- policy options/interventions;
- annual eligible population;
- optional covered lives;
- current treatment/option mix;
- future treatment/option mix;
- evidence and rationale for the population derivation and uptake assumptions;
- the explicit meaning of the annual population series.

Two population bases are supported:

- `annual_eligible_population` — each year is a cross-sectional population relevant to that year's allocation/resource calculation;
- `new_treatment_starts` — each year is a new initiation cohort that may generate consequences in later years.

Current and future shares must sum to one. Invalid shares are not silently normalised.

### Relationship to BIA

Capacity Planning can consume the shared population scenario directly.

BIA currently receives the shared scenario through an explicit **snapshot copy**. The copied BIA population and treatment mix remain editable in BIA. This is intentional: the application does not claim a live link while BIA retains its own population controls, and later BIA edits do not silently mutate the shared scenario.

A future UI refactor may allow BIA to consume the shared scenario live, but that should include explicit lock/synchronisation semantics rather than hidden two-way session-state coupling.

## Resource & Capacity Planning population sources

Resource & Capacity Planning now supports three population sources:

1. **Shared Population & Uptake** — direct use of the reusable population scenario;
2. **Budget Impact Analysis** — reuse the active BIA population and treatment mix;
3. **Define in this workspace** — local population and uptake inputs for a one-off capacity analysis.

This means an evaluator can assess implementation feasibility without entering monetary BIA inputs.

## Resource requirement sources

Three modes are available:

- **Manual** — enter natural-unit use per person directly;
- **Linked clinical model** — derive annual natural-unit profiles from an active Decision Tree, Cohort Markov, or Advanced Markov model;
- **Hybrid** — add manually entered service requirements to the linked clinical resource profile.

Hybrid requirements are summed explicitly. The source of the requirements remains visible in the capacity workspace.

## Clinical resource parameters

Clinical resource linkage uses parameters whose category is explicitly `resource_use`.

Examples include:

- clinician hours/year;
- nurse hours/cycle;
- infusion-chair hours/year;
- scans/event;
- laboratory tests/year;
- admissions or bed-days/year;
- procedures/event;
- device units/event.

A cost parameter is never interpreted as a physical resource automatically. A modeller must create a resource-use parameter and map it to a clinical state or decision-tree node.

## Markov and semi-Markov linkage

For state-transition models, a resource mapping specifies:

- clinical strategy;
- health state;
- capacity resource;
- `resource_use` parameter;
- accrual as `per_year` or `per_cycle`.

The linkage reruns the active clinical model with its selected cycle length, horizon, transition structure and state-accrual convention. Resource units are calculated from state occupancy and then aggregated to annual per-patient profiles.

The clinical cycle must divide one year exactly for annual capacity aggregation, and the clinical horizon must cover the requested capacity horizon. The platform does not silently assume zero resource use beyond a shorter clinical horizon.

## Decision-tree linkage

For decision trees, a resource mapping specifies:

- clinical strategy;
- node;
- capacity resource;
- `resource_use` parameter;
- absolute model time for the resource event.

Branch probabilities determine expected path-weighted resource use. Resource events are assigned to annual capacity periods using their model timing.

## Population-basis safeguard

Longitudinal clinical resource profiles currently require `new_treatment_starts`.

A cross-sectional prevalent/eligible population does not identify the distribution of time since treatment or time in the clinical pathway. Treating that entire stock as a fresh cohort every year would overstate or distort later resource demand. The platform therefore refuses that linkage rather than making the assumption silently.

Manual annual resource requirements remain available for cross-sectional population planning.

A future prevalent-cohort mode should require an explicit distribution of time since initiation/state or an equivalent open-population model.

## Capacity calculation

For each resource and year:

`residual available capacity = total capacity - demand already committed elsewhere`

The planner reports:

- current demand;
- future demand;
- net demand change;
- residual available capacity;
- utilisation;
- headroom;
- shortfall.

Committed other demand cannot exceed total capacity. Invalid inputs are rejected rather than truncated.

## Policy Interpretation handoff

The capacity workspace stores only its **currently validated** result for downstream Policy Interpretation. If the current capacity inputs become invalid, that stored result is cleared.

Policy Interpretation reads the validated capacity result directly. It does not reconstruct feasibility from BIA inputs. Therefore feasibility interpretation works for shared-population, BIA-sourced, and locally defined capacity analyses, including manual, linked-clinical and hybrid resource requirements.

## Methodological boundaries

- A per-patient clinical model does not establish the size of the eligible population.
- Population estimates require separate epidemiological/service evidence and documented assumptions.
- Resource-use parameters should use natural units with explicit evidence and rationale.
- No monetary cost is silently converted into a physical resource quantity.
- No cross-sectional prevalence stock is silently treated as annual new treatment starts.
- Treatment shares are not silently normalised.
- Capacity shortfall is an implementation finding, not an automatic recommendation to ration treatment or expand services.
